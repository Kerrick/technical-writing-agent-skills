# Principles and sources

Each entry gives the principle, then the source. These are from memory of the works. Check details before quoting.

## General craft

- Clutter is the enemy. Cut padding and qualifiers. Know your reader. (Zinsser, *On Writing Well*)
- One sentence at a time, each doing one job. (Klinkenborg, *Several Short Sentences About Writing*)
- Make characters the subjects and actions the verbs. Put old information before new. (Williams, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*)
- The curse of knowledge: experts forget what beginners do not know. This is why expert READMEs fail newcomers. (Pinker, *The Sense of Style*)
- Prefer the concrete word and the short word. Avoid stock phrases and inflated prose. (Orwell, "Politics and the English Language")
- Put stress and emphasis where readers expect them. (Gopen and Swan, "The Science of Scientific Writing")
- Baseline rules of concision, with some dated advice. (Strunk and White, *The Elements of Style*)

## Plain language

- Audience first, short sections, active voice, lists and tables where they fit. (US Federal Plain Language Guidelines, plainlanguage.gov)
- Plain language measurably works. (Kimble, *Writing for Dollars, Writing to Please*)
- People read on the job and on the web in specific ways. Write for that. (Redish, *Letting Go of the Words*)

## Technical writing and documentation

- Writing for a technical reader, with structure and precision. (Zobel, *Writing for Computer Science*; Markel and Selber, *Technical Communication*; Bhatti et al., *Docs for Developers*)
- Tutorials, how-to guides, reference, and explanation are different documents. Do not mix them in one place. A README is mostly a front door that links to each. (Procida, Diataxis, diataxis.fr)
- People want to do things. Cut front matter so they can start, and let them learn by doing. (Carroll, *The Nurnberg Funnel*, minimalism)
- Write each page so it works for a reader who lands on it from search. (Baker, *Every Page Is Page One*)
- Keep docs in the repository and check them like code. (Gentle, *Docs Like Code*)
- Practical house rules for wording and procedures. (Google and Microsoft developer documentation style guides)
- README-specific guidance. (*Art of README*; Make a README; the standard-readme specification)

## Learning and cognition

- Working memory limits what a reader can hold. (Hermans, *The Programmer's Brain*)
- Retention comes from practice and retrieval. (Oakley, *A Mind for Numbers*; Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, *Make It Stick*)
- Worked examples teach novices better than exposition. Put a working example early. (Sweller, cognitive load theory)
- Patterns of how people think about and learn software. (Gardner et al., *Cognitive Patterns*)

## UI and UX writing

- Words are part of the interface. This covers CLI messages and error text. (Metts and Welfle, *Writing Is Designing*; Podmajersky, *Strategic Writing for UX*; Yifrah, *Microcopy*)
- Why instructions fail. (Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*)
- People scan; they do not read. Make headings carry meaning. (Krug, *Don't Make Me Think*; Nielsen Norman Group reading studies)

## Structure and strategy

- Organize so people can find things. (Rosenfeld and Morville, *Information Architecture*)
- Decide who a page is for and what it is for. (Halvorson, *Content Strategy for the Web*)
- Chunk and label information. (Horn, Information Mapping)
