# ADR 004: Each host has its own fetch queue and worker, spaced 10 seconds apart

## Context and Problem Statement

Feeds change at any time and most give no notice. Hosts limit how often one address may ask: on 6 October 2026, three hosts that each serve more than 150 of the maintainer's feeds answered 429 and then blocked the instance for a day, after receiving about 150 requests in 20 seconds every 15 minutes. Inside one process (ADR 001), what fetches feeds, and when?

## Decision Drivers

- A new article shows up within 15 minutes.
- No host receives requests faster than it allows.
- Fetching must fit in the one process.

## Considered Options

1. A queue per host, each with one worker that leaves at least 10 seconds between requests to its host, with hosts fetched side by side
2. One poller that walks every feed in turn, sleeping 10 seconds between two feeds on the same host
3. Subscribing with WebSub, so hosts push new articles

## Decision Outcome

Chosen: option 1, because it is the only one that keeps both the 15-minute driver and the hosts' limits. Option 2 broke the first: against the maintainer's 2,400 feeds, one walk went from 6 minutes to 70. Option 3 left out most feeds: 216 of the 2,400 (9%) advertise a hub.

Confidence: high that requests to one host are spaced, which the test below checks. Low that a new article shows up within 15 minutes on a host with more than 90 feeds, since 90 fetches 10 seconds apart take 15 minutes; each of the hosts that blocked the instance serves more than 150. No walk time or rate of 429s since the change shipped is recorded.

### Consequences

- Good, because a slow host delays only its own feeds.
- Bad, because there is one thread per host with something due: 340 at the peak on the maintainer's instance, against one before, all in the process that also serves the reader (ADR 001).
- Bad, because when fetching stops there is no single loop to look at. `quillfeed fetch-status` lists each host's queue.

### Confirmation

`tests/test_fetch_spacing.py` fails if a host's queue makes two requests less than `SPACING_SECONDS` apart.
