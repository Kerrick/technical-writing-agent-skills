# ADR 004: Each host has its own fetch queue and one worker

## Context and Problem Statement

Feeds change at any time and most give no notice, so Quillfeed polls them. Many feeds share a host: three hosts (two blog platforms and a podcast host) each serve more than 150 of the maintainer's 2,400 feeds. A walk of every feed in turn sent those 150 requests to one host in about 20 seconds, every 15 minutes, and on 6 October all three hosts answered 429 and then blocked the instance's address for a day. Inside one process (ADR 001), what fetches feeds, and when, so that no host is sent a burst?

## Decision Drivers

- A new article shows up within 15 minutes.
- Requests to one host stay within that host's limits.
- Fetching must fit in the one process.

## Considered Options

1. A queue per host, each with one worker that leaves at least 10 seconds between requests to its host, with hosts fetched side by side
2. One poller that walks every feed in turn and sleeps 10 seconds between two feeds on the same host
3. No polling: subscribe with WebSub, so hosts push new articles

## Decision Outcome

Chosen: option 1, because it was the only one of the three that kept both the 15-minute promise and the hosts' limits. Option 2 lost because it breaks the 15-minute promise: run against the maintainer's 2,400 feeds, one walk went from 6 minutes to 70, so a new article could take over an hour to show up. Option 3 lost because only 216 of the 2,400 feeds (9%) advertise a hub, so it leaves out 91% of feeds. How it works is in [doc/design/fetching.md](../design/fetching.md).

Confidence: not recorded by the deciders. It shipped in 0.8.0 on 2 November and runs on the maintainer's instance, where it peaked at 340 worker threads. Nothing recorded shows whether the hosts that blocked the instance have stayed within their limits since.

### Consequences

- Good, because a slow host delays only its own feeds, not every feed after it in a walk.
- Bad, because Quillfeed runs one thread per host with something due, 340 at the peak on the maintainer's instance, against one before, all in the process that also serves the reader (ADR 001).
- Bad, because when fetching stops there is no longer one loop to look at. Whoever operates the instance reads each host's queue with `quillfeed fetch-status`.
- Bad, because at 10 seconds a request, one host's worker fetches at most 90 feeds in 15 minutes. A host with more than 90 feeds due has a round longer than 15 minutes: at least 25 minutes for each of the three hosts with more than 150 feeds.

### Confirmation

`tests/test_fetch_spacing.py` fails if a host's queue sends its second request without first waiting `SPACING_SECONDS`, which `src/quillfeed/fetch.py` sets to 10. Nothing automated checks that the value stays 10, that hosts are fetched side by side, or that a new article shows up within 15 minutes.
